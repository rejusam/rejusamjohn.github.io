ML Explorer · Level 6 · Agents

Memory

A model is given only the messages that fit in its context. See old messages drop out of the window, what that costs, and how a notepad keeps what matters.

About 10 minutes · Every trace and word count is computed in your browser; the rule is in The maths

Overview

In The loop and when to stop every model call was given every message so far. A real model cannot be given an endless list: there is a limit to how much text fits in at once.

This lesson gives the stand-in a budget. Each call is given the task and only the newest messages that fit. A fact found early can drop out before it is used, and then the run has to look it up again. A notepad, one more tool, keeps the facts where later calls can still use them.

You'll learn

  • what the context is, and how a budget sets which messages are in it
  • how a fact that drops out of the window can send a run in circles
  • what a notepad changes, and what it costs

  1. The model is given only its context

    At each step the model is given some text, its context, and writes its next line from what it is given.

    The model on this page is a stand-in. Its choices come from fixed rules, listed in full in What is an agent?, applied to what it is given and nothing else; a real agent makes them with a language model, which can write a wrong or malformed call.

    A language model is given text as tokens (see Tokens), and only so many at once: the tiny model in Large language models, in miniature had a fixed window of characters. Everything the model is given at a step is its context. In this lesson the context is the task and the newest messages that fit in a budget, the window, and in the step on notes also the notes. On this page the size of the context is counted in words, where a word is any piece of text between spaces. That is a simple stand-in for tokens; real models count tokens, which are not the same as words.

    The task: ana and sam each buy 3 of the food they like. What do they pay in total? Here there is no limit, so each call is given every message so far, and the run is done after 6 steps: "That costs $12.90."

    Select a row: the picture shows the window of the model call in that row's step, as a bar of word counts with the newest message on the left. This lesson uses a step limit of 10, not the default of 8; the step on notes says why.

  2. The window

    With a budget, each call is given the task and only the newest messages that fit.

    The rule: the task is always kept, and its own 17 words are not counted. Then the loop goes back from the newest message, adding up words, and keeps each message while the total stays within the budget. The first message that does not fit, and everything older, is left out.

    Move the budget slider, next to Run and Next message, from 6 to 60 words, or tick no limit. The picture gives each kept message's word count and the total. In the trace, the older rows that this call was not given are greyed and tagged "no longer seen"; the rows of the selected step itself, and everything after them, were written by that call or come later.

    For this task, a budget of 54 words or more gives the same 6 steps as no limit. From 8 to 52 words the run goes round until the step limit stops it. At 6 words even ana's result (8 words) does not fit, so the second model call is given only the task, writes the same lookup again, and the repeat check stops the run after 2 steps.

    The maths

    Number the messages after the task m1,…,mtm_1, \dots, m_tm1​,…,mt​, newest last, and let BBB be the budget in words (on this page B is a budget, not a batch). The call is given the task and

    mj,…,mtwith j the smallest index such that∑i=jtwords(mi)≤Bm_j, \dots, m_t \quad\text{with } j \text{ the smallest index such that}\quad \sum_{i=j}^{t} \text{words}(m_i) \le Bmj​,…,mt​with j the smallest index such thati=j∑t​words(mi​)≤B

    Every message has at least one word, so the sum grows as jjj goes back. Taking the smallest such jjj is the same as stopping at the first message that does not fit. If even mtm_tmt​ does not fit, no jjj qualifies and the call is given only the task. With no limit, B=∞B = \inftyB=∞ and every message is kept.

  3. A dropped fact costs steps

    At 12 words, a fact found early drops out of the window before it is used, so the stand-in writes the lookup again.

    Step 1 writes lookup("ana") and gets "ana likes apples and lives by the sea.", 8 words. Step 2 is given that result and writes lookup("apples").

    Opened directly, the trace selects step 3's plan line. That step's call is given only the newest messages: 4 + 1 + 7 = 12 words, the whole budget. ana's result would take 12 + 8 = 20, so it is left out, greyed with everything older. ana's food is no longer in the messages it is given, so the stand-in applies R3 again and writes lookup("ana").

    From then on the run takes turns: lookup("ana"), lookup("apples"), and so on until the step limit stops it after 10 steps with no answer. In all it writes lookup("ana") 5 times and lookup("apples") 5 times. sam is never looked up.

    The repeat check from The loop and when to stop does not catch this. It compares a call only with the one just before it, and here no two calls in a row are the same.

  4. Notes

    With the notepad on, the stand-in writes each fact in the notes, and the notes are given to every later call.

    The notepad turns on R2: if the last result is a fact not yet in the notes, write it in the notes. So each lookup is followed by a step that writes its result down. The budget is still 12 words.

    Each call is given its window and all the notes, so the notes are part of its context, but they sit outside the window: their words are not counted against the budget. That is a choice made for this page; in other designs the notes take up part of the budget too.

    The run is done after 10 steps with 4 notes: "That costs $12.90." Step 9's window holds none of the lookup results; the facts come from the notes, and it writes the calculator call. Switch the notepad off to see the circling run again.

    The notes cost steps: one per note, 4 more than the 6 with no limit. That is why this lesson uses a step limit of 10; at the usual 8, the notepad run stops at the limit. With the notepad, every budget on the slider from 8 words up finishes. At 6 words ana's result drops out before the step that would write it down.

  5. Real agents

    A real model's context is limited too, so agents built around one often keep notes or summaries.

    A real language model can be given only so many tokens at once. Agents built around one therefore often keep what matters outside the window, as notes, or replace older messages with a short summary of them.

    Pick a run from the run menu. The picture puts the three runs of this lesson side by side, and the trace shows the one you picked.

    Notes are not free: here each one cost a step, and a note holds only what was written in it.

Quick check

At a budget of 12 words, why did the agent look ana up again?

Right. Step 3's call was given 4 + 1 + 7 = 12 words of newer messages; ana's result would have made 12 + 8 = 20.

lookup gives the same text every time: "ana likes apples and lives by the sea." ana was looked up again because the first result was no longer in the window.

The step limit only stops a run; it does not choose calls. ana was looked up again at step 3 of 10.

The repeat check only stops a run; it never writes a call. It did not fire here either, since no two calls in a row were the same.

What does the notepad change?

Right. At 12 words the notepad run is done after 10 steps with 4 notes.

The budget stays at 12 words. On this page the notes are kept outside the window, a choice made for this page.

It adds steps, one per note: 10 instead of 6 with no limit.

At 6 words ana's result drops out before it can be written down, and the repeat check stops the run after 2 steps.

Lesson complete

  • A model is given only its context: here the task and the newest messages that fit in a budget of words.
  • A fact that drops out of the window has to be found again; at 12 words the run went in circles until the step limit.
  • A notepad keeps facts for later calls, at the cost of a step per note.

Common misconceptions

  • "The model is given everything earlier in the run." Only what fits in its window: at 12 words, step 3's call was not given ana's result.
  • "Words and tokens are the same." Words here are a simple stand-in; real models count tokens.
  • "A notepad is free." Each note cost one step: 4 notes, 4 extra steps.
