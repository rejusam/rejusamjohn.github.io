Machine learning, one idea at a time

ML Explorer

How machine learning works, for students and complete beginners: from what a model is to fitting one, testing it fairly and keeping it from overfitting, and on to the classic methods: nearest neighbours, trees and forests, clustering and PCA.

Each lesson shows one idea on a single picture, in a few short steps. Move the controls and watch what changes. Every number in the pictures is computed in your browser from the lesson’s data as you go.

The main text needs no maths. The formulas, and the reasons behind them, sit one click deeper under “The maths”.

The path

Each lesson builds on the ones before it. L0 to L3 are ready; L4 to L6 are coming next. Your progress is kept in this browser only.

  1. L0

    Start here

    1. Lesson 1 · About 8 min

      What is a model?

      A model is a rule learned from examples. Once it has learned, it can make a guess about a case it has never seen.

  2. L1

    Data & intuition

    1. Lesson 2 · About 10 min

      Data as numbers

      A model only ever sees numbers. Each example becomes a short list of numbers, a vector, which is also a point you can draw and measure.

    2. Lesson 3 · About 12 min

      Centre and spread

      Two numbers can sum up a set of values: where they sit, and how far they spread.

    3. Lesson 4 · About 12 min

      Measuring error

      Every model misses. A loss sums up all the misses in one number.

    4. Lesson 5 · About 10 min

      Slopes and derivatives

      A slope says how steep a curve is at one point, and which way is downhill.

  3. L2

    Learning from data

    1. Lesson 6 · About 12 min

      Linear regression

      Least squares chooses the straight line with the smallest total squared error.

    2. Lesson 7 · About 12 min

      Gradient descent

      Gradient descent finds the bottom of a loss by taking small steps downhill, again and again.

    3. Lesson 8 · About 12 min

      Many features and the loss surface

      With two numbers to learn, the loss becomes a surface. Its shape decides how easily gradient descent finds the bottom.

    4. Lesson 9 · About 10 min

      Train, validate, test

      A model is only useful if it works on data it has not seen. Here is how to measure that honestly.

    5. Lesson 10 · About 12 min

      Overfitting and regularisation

      A model can be too simple or too flexible. Both do badly on new data, for different reasons.

  4. L3

    Classic ML

    1. Lesson 11 · About 10 min

      k-nearest neighbours

      To label a new case, look at the cases most like it and let them vote.

    2. Lesson 12 · About 12 min

      Logistic regression

      Squash a straight-line score into a probability, then pick a threshold to turn it into a class.

    3. Lesson 13 · About 12 min

      Naive Bayes

      Describe each class on its own, then let Bayes' rule say which class a new case most likely came from.

    4. Lesson 14 · About 12 min

      Support vector machines

      Of all the straight lines that split two classes, pick the one with the widest empty street around it.

    5. Lesson 15 · About 10 min

      Decision trees

      Label a case by asking it a few yes/no questions, each one chosen to sort the classes apart as well as it can.

    6. Lesson 16 · About 10 min

      Bagging and random forests

      One tree is jumpy. Many trees, each grown on its own sample of the data, voting together, are steadier, and the rows each tree never saw give you a test score for free.

    7. Lesson 17 · About 12 min

      Gradient boosting

      Add small trees one at a time, each fitted to what the model still gets wrong.

    8. Lesson 18 · About 10 min

      k-means clustering

      With no labels at all, find groups by moving a few centres until they settle.

    9. Lesson 19 · About 12 min

      Principal component analysis

      Find the direction in which the data vary most, and keep most of the information with fewer numbers.
